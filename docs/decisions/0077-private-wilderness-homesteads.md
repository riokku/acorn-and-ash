# Private wilderness homesteads

Chris requested construction within a tier-dependent radius around each home,
visible while building. The shared starter clearing cannot accommodate several
large plots, so Chris approved homes in suitable wilderness clearings as well.

A first tent establishes a private area with radius 12 m. Teepees expand it to
18 m, cabins to 26 m and larger cabins to 36 m, keeping the same center. New
plots and upgrades must not overlap another home area or protected discovery
and encounter clearings, and the entire area must fit inside the world. Existing
homes and placed objects remain intact; new construction still respects other
players' boundaries when older homes overlap. Visitors cannot build in a plot.

The server checks the entire object footprint, including rotated fence ends,
before spending materials. Both ends and width must fit inside the owner's
area. Wilderness trees, rocks, water and uneven foundations refuse placement.
The preview uses the same rules and intersects the actual terrain. Placed art,
house collision and camera blockers use the ground height at their center.

While the build menu or preview is open, a ground-following ribbon and minimap
outline show the area, with a clear radius label. An upgrade previews its proposed
larger area. Normal play hides this overlay. Area boundaries are derived from
persisted homes; no new storage table or migration is needed. Newly placed
campfires, fences and paths also save their owner, preserving public use of fires.

A player whose upgrade overlaps a neighbor must leave that upgrade waiting.
Moving or abandoning an established home needs a separate design; this change
does not silently relocate homes or remove existing belongings.
