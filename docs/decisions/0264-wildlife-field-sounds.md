# Wildlife field sounds

Make nearby animals discoverable by listening as well as looking. Drive quiet
hoofbeats and brush movement from interpolated motion, and a short elk snort from
the existing tense pose. These are synthesized woodland cues, not recordings.
They fade over distance and pan relative to the camera, using the existing forest
sound bus and the player's sound controls.

Do not change prey awareness, threat rules or network messages. Stationary animals
stay quiet. Arrival, correction snaps, respawning, hidden tabs, indoor spaces and
long frames must not create catch-up sounds. Remove history when an animal leaves
interest. Bound requests per frame and reuse the existing disposable voice pool.

This helps players notice an animal moving in nearby undergrowth without adding
constant calls, markers or a new task. Client scheduling is cosmetic only.
