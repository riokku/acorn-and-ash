# 0115 · Conifers redrawn in a chunky, storybook style

## Context

Chris wants the forest to feel cozy and hand-painted, in the spirit of Northgard, while staying a Pacific Northwest forest. The Douglas-fir, western redcedar and Sitka spruce from decision 0069 were thin sprays on a pole, generated by a script that is not modelled in Blender, and they read as sparse from the player's camera.

## Decision

- The three conifers are rebuilt in Blender (`tools/art/trees.py`, `assets/trees/trees.blend`) as stacked tiers of chunky, spade-shaped boughs. Each bough has a bright, warm top that catches the light and a deep shadowed underside, so the crown reads as soft layered masses from far away.
- The recipe is vertex colours only, like the iron axe and the forest-floor plants: a darker, cooler green near the trunk, brighter warm tips, and a flared, reddish trunk (cedar) or darker furrowed one (fir) or greyer one (spruce).
- The files keep their names, heights (14, 12 and 16 m) and the two-part layout (`Bark` and `Leaves`), so the game loads them the same way (plus a small change to pick a shape for each tree), bark is still painted by the game and the leaves still change colour with the seasons. `tools/check-pnw-trees.mjs` still checks this.
- Each species comes in three shapes (the plain name, then `-b` and `-c`), nine in all: a slim tall-trunked and a broad old fir, an upright and a wide sweeping cedar, a narrow spire and a broad mature spruce. Detailed trees are 2,039–3,554 triangles and the distant versions 467–746, inside the 4,000 and 800 budgets.
- The old generator `tools/build-pnw-trees.mjs` is removed. Running it would overwrite the new art.
- The Bark and Leaves materials have different roughness on purpose. Otherwise the glTF import step merges them into one and the game can no longer tell them apart.

## Consequences

- Which shape a tree wears depends only on its id (`treeVariant` in `prop-models.ts`), so it never changes while the tree is shaking, falling, regrowing or switching between near and distant models. Nothing is saved for it. Trees of the same kind and shape are still drawn in one batch, so this adds no draw calls per tree.
- Existing worlds get the new look with no save change. Collision, stumps and falling use the same dimensions as before.
- Each species clears the ground differently (fir on a tall bare trunk, spruce a little lower, cedar with a low skirt), and the lowest tier is patchy so the trunk shows through.
- The new rows in `assets/LICENSES.csv` are CC0-1.0, as the old trees were (Chris confirmed).
- Trees only change the look. Next in the series: the deciduous trees (big-leaf maple, red alder) to replace the older birch and oak art, then mossy logs and stumps.
