# Visual audit: cabin → forest → skeleton → home

Baseline source: `de60bcab67cd9acb4e1088ae4ee163cb2d03cc3f` (main). This audit distinguishes source inspection, historical repository images and fresh rendered evidence. Historical images identify questions; they do not prove the current build has the same issue. Nothing below certifies motion, sound quality or performance without a corresponding observed run.

## First focused improvement

**P1: keep the camera out of evergreen foliage.** A controlled review of the Cedar trail showed a solid foreground branch wedge hiding the nearby path. Existing camera blockers represented tree trunks but omitted the broader canopy. Adding camera-only canopy proxies gives a clearly visible improvement at the reproduced trail pose while preserving the rendered trees, player collision and orbit controls.

This is a bounded camera fix. It does not guarantee an unobstructed view through every surrounding branch: a second walking position retains a large off-center crown. That remaining obstruction is P2 follow-up, not a claimed result of this change.

The earlier daylight color trial was too subtle to establish a useful player-facing improvement. Further lighting changes are deferred and excluded from this PR. Preserve established night visibility, material colors, fog, fire behavior and weather transitions.

## Evidence and review limits

Fresh baseline galleries cover cabin day/night, trees, furnished home and skeleton lineup. They establish coherent timber construction, distinct evergreen/broadleaf silhouettes, readable ivory/burgundy/teal skeleton materials and an existing strong fire accent. They do not establish live encounter timing or navigation. Historical images under `docs/previews/` supplied the initial questions in the table, not matched proof of a current defect.

The generic gallery applies season after its daylight update, so its summer materials appear under the untinted base light rig. It is not proof of summer gameplay lighting. The controlled game-camera fixture applies the game's seasonal lighting and uses actual game assets and the follow camera.

The default player appears amber/gold. Source inspection confirms the local character uses the supplied identity independently of the remote-player roster and applies the selected tint across its materials. This appearance is not evidence of a skin-lighting defect or a missing model. No claim covers all six cosmetic bodies.

All route captures below are **controlled game-camera reviews using a local simulation fixture**, not a live online playthrough. The initial camera before/after pairs match player position, view, world geometry, season, time and weather; follow-up sections describe their own capture conditions and differences. Particle and idle-animation phases can differ. No claim is made about online persistence, sound quality, a completed skeleton encounter or overall performance from these stills.

## Follow-up review after PR #340

Reviewed baseline: `dd89812` (merged main). The review retained one focused presentation change: omit the local player's own floating name so it cannot overlap enemy identification. Camera and foliage trials below did not establish an improvement and were not retained.

**Walking destination:** the right-hand crown dominates part of the frame, but the full player, feet and central route remain visible. A verified trial added a 0.2 m vertical clearance margin, scaled horizontally by aspect ratio, while retaining the original collision probes. Matched renders showed no meaningful opening beside the player. The trial was rejected and its additional raycasts were removed.

A diagnostic against the rendered instanced tree geometry found the first sampled leaf intersections approximately 6.38–7.32 m from the camera, with further leaf layers behind them. These are off-axis radial distances, not forward depth. They place the sampled crown around the player's depth, rather than immediately beside the camera. CPU raycasts omit shader wind and texture alpha, so these measurements are approximate. They nevertheless rule out the proposed small, roughly 3 m camera-local cutaway as a solution to this view. No cutaway was implemented. Extending it through nearby trees would change forest visibility to improve one composition without an established gameplay benefit.

**Validated home fixture:** the cabin at (-2, -4.2) is upgraded through the simulation's build request and checked before capture. Departure faces the forest from outside the home; return faces the cabin. These six sampled poses retain the full player and readable nearby ground. The return views clearly identify the roof, door, chimney and log walls. At night the existing warm window draws the eye to home; the player is dark but remains distinguishable from the ground. Forced-storm rain adds visible streaks without masking the player or cabin. No relighting or additional window glow is justified by these samples.

## Next review priorities

Reproduce the cropped stress pose through ordinary movement and orbit, recording camera distance and the actual blocking scenery; a demonstrated camera failure there would justify a narrower implementation than globally fading forest foliage. Observe threat timing in motion, including night and adverse weather; the partly obscured weapon remains an encounter-readability question. Preserve the home framing and existing fire-dependent warmth established above. Treat the walking endpoint's crowded crown as a composition question unless movement or encounters demonstrate repeated loss of useful visibility; do not erase nearby scenery merely to clear that frame. The audit table identifies further questions, not a mandate for changes without observed benefit.
